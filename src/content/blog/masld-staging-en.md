Working on liver transcriptomes, I kept hitting labels — NAFLD, NASH, MASLD, MASH, and stage labels like F2 and S3 — that get used as if they were one scale and are not. The four acronyms are non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), and their replacements metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH).

Fat can accumulate inside liver cells; that is steatosis. On its own it is usually harmless and reversible. Sometimes it also causes inflammation and cell death, and the liver responds the way any tissue does — it lays down scar tissue, and the build-up of that scar is fibrosis. Scar does not do the liver's job and does not go away easily, and enough of it stiffens and remodels the organ. So the disease has two separate things to measure: **how much fat**, and **how much scar**. They are graded on different scales, they move independently, and only one of them tells you how the patient will do.

## The rename, and why it was not cosmetic

In 2023 a multi-society consensus replaced the old names.

| Old | New | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NAFLD | **MASLD** | steatotic liver disease plus at least one cardiometabolic risk factor |
| NASH | **MASH** | the same, plus inflammation and hepatocyte injury on histology |
| — | **MetALD** (metabolic dysfunction and alcohol-related liver disease) | MASLD in someone also drinking moderately |

<mark>"Non-alcoholic" defined the disease by what it was not.</mark> A diagnosis of exclusion is awkward to study and worse to code: patients who drank a little fell out of the category without belonging to the alcohol one either. MASLD defines the disease by what it **is** — fat in the liver together with metabolic dysfunction — and MetALD gives the people in between a name.

The **cardiometabolic risk factors**, any one of which qualifies:

1. BMI (body mass index) ≥ 25 kg/m² (≥ 23 for Asian populations), or waist circumference above the sex- and ethnicity-adjusted threshold
2. Fasting glucose ≥ 100 mg/dL, or HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin) ≥ 5.7%, or type 2 diabetes, or treatment for it
3. Blood pressure ≥ 130/85 mmHg, or antihypertensive treatment
4. Triglycerides ≥ 150 mg/dL, or lipid-lowering treatment
5. HDL (high-density lipoprotein) cholesterol ≤ 40 mg/dL (males) or ≤ 50 (females), or lipid-lowering treatment

**MetALD is a gradient, not a box.** The same label covers MASLD-predominant and ALD (alcohol-associated liver disease)-predominant patients depending on where they sit on the alcohol axis, which matters if you are stratifying on it.

## Two axes, staged separately

The part that trips people up: fat and scar are scored on different scales and move independently.

**Steatosis grade (S0–S3)** — what fraction of liver cells contain fat. Read from a biopsy under a microscope:

| Grade | Hepatocytes with fat |
| --- | --- |
| S0 | &lt; 5% |
| S1 | 5–33% |
| S2 | >33–66% |
| S3 | &gt; 66% |

**Fibrosis stage (F0–F4)** — how much scar tissue there is and, just as importantly, where. Collagen is what scar is made of:

| Stage | Where the collagen is |
| --- | --- |
| F0 | none |
| F1 | perisinusoidal, starting in zone 3 (F1c is portal only) |
| F2 | reaching the portal areas |
| F3 | **bridging** — scars connect portal areas to each other |
| F4 | cirrhosis; the architecture itself is remodelled |

The zonation matters here. F1 appears around the central vein because that is [zone 3](../liver-cells-en/), where fat accumulates and oxygen is lowest. Fibrosis starts where the damage starts.

## Only one axis predicts outcome

<mark class="blue">Fibrosis stage predicts liver-related mortality. Steatosis grade essentially does not.</mark>

A patient can sit at S3 F0 — a liver two-thirds full of fat, no scarring — for years without liver-related consequence. Risk rises sharply from **F2** onwards and most of it sits at F3–F4. That is why trials are powered on fibrosis stage, why non-invasive scores aim at fibrosis, and why "how much fat" matters less for prognosis.

MASH is the mechanism in between. Fat alone sits there; fat **plus inflammation and dying liver cells** is what keeps the scarring going. Which is why the names separate them: MASLD is the fat, MASH is the fat doing damage.

## What this does to an analysis

- **"MASLD versus control" averages S3 F0 with S1 F3.** The control group here is non-diseased liver, but the disease group is not one thing: two very different biologies land in it, and the differential expression result is their mean. Check the stage before trusting it.
- **Stratify on fibrosis, not on diagnosis**, when the question is about progression. A comparison across F0→F4 is a comparison along the axis that matters; a comparison across S0→S3 — the fat axis — often is not.
- **Old and new labels coexist in public data.** A GEO (Gene Expression Omnibus) series from 2019 says NAFLD and NASH; one from 2025 says MASLD and MASH. They are not interchangeable — MASLD requires a cardiometabolic risk factor that the NAFLD cohort was never screened for, so a re-labelled old cohort is an approximation.
- **Histology is the staging source, and most samples do not have it.** Where stage is absent, non-invasive surrogates appear instead — the FIB-4 (fibrosis-4) index, elastography — and they are not the same variable.

## Summary

- 2023 renamed the disease by what it is: steatosis plus a cardiometabolic risk factor = MASLD
- **MASH** adds inflammation and hepatocyte injury; **MetALD** covers moderate drinkers and is a gradient rather than a category
- Any **one** of five cardiometabolic risk factors qualifies
- Two axes are staged separately: **steatosis S0–S3**, **fibrosis F0–F4**
- Fibrosis starts in zone 3, where the fat and the low oxygen are
- **Fibrosis stage predicts outcome; steatosis grade largely does not** — risk climbs from F2
- A "disease versus control" comparison mixes stages; stratify on fibrosis, and check whether the labels are the old vocabulary or the new
